Geophysical Fluid Dynamics

At the Niels Bohr Institute, I was a teacher's assistant three times: once for the graduate course Computational Atmosphere and Ocean Dynamics (2022) attended by 10 students and twice for the undergraduate course Fluid Dynamics (2022, 2023) attended by 70 students.

My responsibilities included:

  • Developing numerical exercises (e.g. Shallow water model exercise and Sverdrup exercise);
  • Introducing the exercises and guiding the students through them;
  • Grading and joint feedback.

I also gave a guest lecture on turbulence in Fluid Dynamics 2025.

Near-Inertial Waves

In collaboration with the outreach team of the Next Generation of Earth Modeling Systems project, I took part in reframing my research on near-inertial wave-induced mixing to reach the wider scientific community.

The two outreach materials included:

  • A video where the research topic is introduced and placed in the wider context of the project;
  • A science explainer outlining the main challenges and novelties in our research.

Ungdomslaboratoriet (ULAB)

Over the course of four years at ULAB, I organized physics lab exercises for hundreds of students across a wide range of ages (middle school, high school and exchange university students). The topics included radiation, greenhouse gases, exoplanets, electromagnetism and more. I developed a brochure that advertised our work to teachers across Denmark.

What this experience taught me:

  • Collaboration in a team where everyone feels valued and supported;
  • Reaching audiences of varying experience levels;
  • Creative ways of teaching physics;
  • The joy of engaging younger people in science.

ULAB was one of my favorite activities I did at the Niels Bohr Institute. Our visitors were always full of curiosity, and the team of young scientists that I had the pleasure of working with continuously inspired me and some remain friends to this day.
